FPV Car
Build Guide

A small car you drive from your phone while watching live video from a camera on the car. No app needed, it runs in the phone's browser.

Made for Pranav · 10 October 2026 · Version 2 (power bank only)

Read this first The part names in this guide were read from the small pictures in the Amazon order list, not from the product pages. When each part arrives, check its label against the table in section 2. If the motor driver is not a TB6612FNG, the wiring in section 4 changes.

1How it works

  1. The ESP32-CAM board makes its own Wi-Fi hotspot.
  2. Your phone joins that hotspot and opens a web page stored on the board.
  3. The page shows the camera video and has drive buttons (forward, back, left, right, stop).
  4. When you press a button, the ESP32-CAM tells the motor driver which way to spin the wheels.
  5. The motor driver feeds power-bank power to the motors. The ESP32-CAM cannot do this itself because its pins are far too weak to run a motor.

3Power plan

The power bank runs the whole car. Its 5 V goes to two places: the ESP32-CAM and the motor driver. No AA cells are needed.

  • Check the output rating printed on the power bank. It should say 5 V at 2 A or more.
  • Speed: the motors get 5 V, so the car is a little slower than it would be on 6 V. Still fine for a light car.
  • Switching off: unplug the power bank.
Fit the capacitor When the motors start or change direction they pull a burst of current, and the 5 V dips for a moment. That dip can freeze the video or restart the ESP32-CAM. A capacitor soldered across the ESP32-CAM's 5V and GND pins works like a tiny reserve tank and covers the dip. The leg next to the stripe is minus and goes to GND. Fitted backwards, it can pop.

Getting 5 V out of the power bank

Neither the ESP32-CAM nor the motor driver has a USB port. Cut an old USB cable that fits the power bank, and find the red (+5 V) and black (GND) wires inside. Check with the multimeter that red really is +5 V. Those two wires feed the whole car.

Your power bank looked like the kind with a built-in cable. If it has no ordinary USB socket, you will need a small adapter or socket that its plug fits into. Check this the day it arrives.

If the video still freezes when you drive

Then the motors need their own battery. Move the driver's VM wire from the power bank to the plus of a 4 × AA alkaline pack (regular Duracell is fine), and join the pack's minus to GND. Everything else stays the same.

If your kit has four motors Join the two left motors together (in parallel) on AO1/AO2 and the two right motors on BO1/BO2. The driver handles about 1.2 A per side continuously, so do not hold the wheels still by hand while it is driving.
Three things that kill boards Plus and minus swapped anywhere. The capacitor fitted backwards. A blob of solder joining two pins. Check each with the multimeter before the first power-on (section 7).

These four pins were chosen because they are the usual free pins on an ESP32-CAM when the memory-card slot is not used. So do not put an SD card in the board for this car.

5Day-by-day plan

The parts arrive over ten days. This order lets you do something useful each time a parcel lands.

Sat 10 OctESP32 board, multimeter
  • Install the Arduino IDE on the laptop and add the ESP32 boards package.
  • Plug in the ESP32 dev board and upload the Blink example. This proves the laptop, cable and software all work.
  • Learn two multimeter jobs: DC voltage (measure any battery) and continuity (the beep mode that tells you two points are joined).
Sun 11 OctChassis, power bank
  • Assemble the chassis.
  • Solder two wires to each motor. Do it quickly: the motor tabs are thin and break if bent or overheated.
  • Check the power bank's plug and its output rating (5 V at 2 A or more).
  • Cut the USB cable and confirm 5 V on the red and black wires with the multimeter.
  • Touch those two wires to each motor to see it spin.
  • Buy the capacitor from the market.
Fri 16 OctESP32-CAM
  • Either wait for the programmer base on the 18th, or flash it now through the ESP32 dev board (section 6).
  • Load the camera test program and watch the video on your phone. Getting video first, with no motors, makes later problems easier to find.
Sun 18 OctProgrammer base
  • Plug the ESP32-CAM into the base. From now on, loading code is just a USB cable.
  • Load the full car program. The video and buttons will work; the wheels will not, because the driver is not here yet.
Tue 20 OctMotor driver
  • Solder the header pins onto the driver.
  • Wire everything as in section 4, including the capacitor.
  • Do the first power-on checks (section 7) with the wheels off the ground.
  • Drive.

6Software

What the program does

  • Starts a Wi-Fi hotspot, for example named FPV-Car.
  • Serves one web page at 192.168.4.1 with the live video and the drive buttons.
  • Stops the motors by itself if the phone disconnects, so the car cannot run away.

Arduino IDE settings for the ESP32-CAM

  • Board: AI Thinker ESP32-CAM
  • Port: the one that appears when you plug the board in

Flashing before the programmer arrives (optional)

The ESP32 dev board can act as a USB adapter for the ESP32-CAM. This is a common trick, but skip it if it feels fiddly and just wait for the 18th.

Then join IO0 to GND on the ESP32-CAM, press its reset button, and upload. When the upload finishes, remove the IO0 wire and press reset again to run the program.

The car program itself is not written yet. It is the next job, and it is easier to get right once the real parts are confirmed.

7First power-on checks

Do these in order, with the multimeter, before putting the car on the floor.

  1. Nothing connected to power. In beep mode, check that 5V and GND do not keep beeping together. A steady beep means a short. A very short beep that stops is just the capacitor filling up.
  2. Capacitor. Look again: the striped leg goes to GND.
  3. Power bank cable alone. It should read about 5 V. Note which wire is plus.
  4. Lift the car so the wheels are in the air, then plug in the power bank. A short twitch of the wheels at power-on is normal.
  5. Measure. The ESP32-CAM 5V pin and the driver VM pin should both read about 5 V. The 3V3 pin should read about 3.3 V. The video should work on the phone.
  6. Press Forward. Both sides should spin forward. If one side goes backward, swap that motor's two wires.
  7. Press Left and Right. If they are reversed, swap the left and right motor connections on the driver.
  8. Put it on the floor and drive.

8Problems and fixes

What you seeLikely cause and fix
Video freezes or the board restarts when the car starts movingPower dip. Check the capacitor is fitted and the power wires are short and firmly joined. If it keeps happening, give the motors their own AA pack (section 3).
Video works but the wheels do nothingCheck the ground wire to the driver. Check STBY, PWMA and PWMB are at 3.3 V. Check VM reads about 5 V.
Only one side worksA loose signal wire or a cold solder joint on that side of the driver.
Car is slow or stops after a few minutesPower bank is low or too weak. Measure VM while the motors run: if it drops well below 4.5 V, charge the power bank or try a stronger one.
Code will not uploadIO0 must be joined to GND during upload (the programmer base does this for you). Try pressing reset just as the upload starts. Try another USB cable: many are charge-only.
Camera error in the serial logThe camera's ribbon cable is not fully seated. Lift the black latch, push the ribbon in straight, and close the latch.
Power bank switches itself offSome power banks cut out when the load is small. Leaving the video streaming usually keeps it awake.
Phone drops off the car's Wi-FiThe phone leaves hotspots that have no internet. Turn off mobile data or choose "stay connected" when asked.

9What to expect, and later upgrades

  • Video: small and a bit laggy compared with real FPV goggles. Fine for driving around a room slowly; not for racing.
  • Range: roughly one or two rooms. The ESP32-CAM's built-in antenna is small.
  • Speed: walking pace. TT motors are slow but have enough pull for a light car.

Good next steps once it drives:

  • Speed slider on the web page.
  • Headlight using the ESP32-CAM's built-in flash LED.
  • External antenna for more range.
  • A separate battery pack for the motors, for more speed and steadier video.
  • Use the spare ESP32 dev board for sensors, for example a distance sensor that stops the car before it hits a wall.
